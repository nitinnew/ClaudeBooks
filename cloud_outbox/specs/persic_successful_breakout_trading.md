## 1. The method in brief

A 30-page practitioner's guide to one technique: buy the breakout above horizontal or trend-line resistance of a daily continuation pattern (ascending triangle, rectangle, symmetrical triangle, flag, pennant, flat base or any tight consolidation) in a stock that is in an uptrend, preferably when an intraday chart (5–60 min) shows a pattern with the **same breakout level** ("time frame confluence") [p. 5–14]. Entry is a buy-stop just above resistance; the initial stop sits just below the pattern's rising support line; half the position is sold at a first target (rising resistance line or Fibonacci extension) and the rest at a second Fibonacci target or a trailing stop placed under each new consolidation [p. 19–26]. The author calls his rule "UP or OUT": if price does not accelerate immediately after entry, he sells half [p. 30]. Short-term (1–20 days), US stocks, sized up in trending markets and down (or to cash) in corrections [p. 4, 15].

Evidence: two worked trades (CQB +10% [p. 26]; MUR failed breakout, loss minimised [p. 31–32]) and the author's "fifteen years of trading experience" [p. 4]. No statistics.

**Pseudocode** (daily bars only; intraday confluence cannot be tested — see Section 5)

```
universe_t: 2-equivalent <= C_t <= 2000-equivalent (see Section 5), SMA(V,63) >= liquidity floor, C_t/C_{t-21} > 1
pattern (approximation of "consolidation below horizontal resistance"):
  R = max(H_{t-n..t}), n in [7, 60]                                   # ASSUMPTION: base length 7–60 sessions
  touches of R (H >= 0.99*R) >= 2
  base depth (R - min(L_{t-n..t}))/R <= 0.15                          # ASSUMPTION: "tight"
  rising support: linear fit of the base's swing lows has slope >= 0  # ascending triangle / flag-like; rectangle allowed (slope ~0)
  BB(20,2) width at t-1 in lowest 25% of last 120 days               # contraction (ASSUMPTION threshold)
  no earnings within next 10 sessions (needs calendar; else skip filter and flag)
order: buy-stop at R*1.002 (≈ the book's few cents above), valid while L > stop_level; cancel if L <= stop_level first
stop_level = min(support line at t, last swing low) * 0.99            # "slightly below" support
fill: if O >= entry_level then fill at O else entry_level
targets: swing = (most recent swing high Hs) - (prior swing low Ls); retrace point P = lowest low after Hs
         T1 = P + 1.000*swing (or rising resistance line, if lower);  T2 = P + 1.618*swing
day-of-entry and next day: if C_entry_day < entry*1.01 and day+1 close < day+1 open → sell 50% at close ("UP or OUT", ASSUMPTION translation)
at T1: sell 50%; keep stop
trail remaining: after a new 5-day consolidation (range <= 4%) is broken to a new high, stop = min(L of that consolidation)*0.99
exit remainder at T2, trail, or after 20 sessions                    # holding-period cap from "1-20 days"
size: shares = floor(max_loss / (entry - stop_level)); max_loss = 1% equity (ASSUMPTION, equation missing);
      cap 25% of equity (15% if index proxy < SMA50)
```

**Ambiguities & assumptions**

- **Pattern recognition is explicitly human**: "This flexibility sets humans apart from computers -- so far." [p. 11]. The coded approximation (consolidation box with ≥ 2 touches, rising lows) will diverge from the author's chart selection.
- **Intraday confluence** (same breakout level on a 5–60 min chart) [p. 12–14, 19] cannot be tested on daily data. ASSUMPTION: drop it; note that this removes the author's main quality filter.
- **Position-size equation** is an image and missing from the text [p. 22]; the text says only to trade as many shares as keep the stop-out loss within your maximum. ASSUMPTION: 1% of equity per trade.
- **"UP or OUT"** is judged intraday within hours [p. 23, 30]. ASSUMPTION: if the entry day closes less than 1% above entry, or day 2 is a down day, sell half.
- **Fibonacci extension** text differs: "1.00 Fibonacci extension of the move from the 'Most recent swing low' to the 'Most recent swing high.'" [p. 20–21] (CQB) vs first target ~1.0 and main target at 1.618 projected from the retracement point (MUR) [p. 29]. ASSUMPTION: T1 = 1.0, T2 = 1.618, both projected from the retracement low.
- **Market regime** is described only through an S&P 500 diagram (image) [p. 16]. ASSUMPTION: index proxy above its 50-day SMA = trending; below = reduce max position to 15%.
- **Volume confirmation** is optional when pattern and market are strong [p. 16]. ASSUMPTION: base case no volume filter; variant requires breakout-day volume > 1.5× 50-day average.

## 3. Risk & money-management rules

- Max 25% of the portfolio in one stock; reduce to 10–15% in corrections and range-bound markets, or stay in cash [p. 21–22].
- Re-check market conditions just before placing the order [p. 21].
- Size so a stop-out loses no more than your maximum single-trade loss (equation not in text) [p. 22].
- Kelly sizing as an option: W = winners ÷ total trades, R = average gain ÷ average loss; Kelly % gives the position size (e.g. 0.15 → 15% per position) [p. 22]. The formula itself is an image.
- Increase exposure in uptrends, scale back in range-bound markets/corrections; focus on defensive or uncorrelated stocks in uncertain markets [p. 15].
- Avoid holding through earnings and binary (FDA) events [p. 17].

## 4. Non-codable guidance

- Check news, upcoming company events, message boards and basic fundamentals before buying [p. 17–18].
- Trade entry is "the most important point"; watch the reaction right after the breakout [p. 23, 30].
- Flexible interpretation of trend lines; add any clear consolidation with a clear breakout point to the watch list [p. 9, 11].
- Confirm an uptrend continues: each new low and consolidation should be higher than the last [p. 25, 31].

## 5. Adapting to NSE (Indian equities)

- **Data required:** daily OHLCV; Bollinger Bands(20,2); swing pivots for Fibonacci targets; an index proxy for regime; earnings dates (filter); intraday bars (5–60 min) for the confluence rule.
- **Testability with our data:**
  - Backtestable 2005–2026 on daily prices: universe filters, consolidation breakout with buy-stop entry, stop below support, Fibonacci targets, half-exit and trailing rules (with the "UP or OUT" rule approximated on daily bars).
  - Not testable: intraday confluence (no intraday data), earnings-proximity filter (no earnings calendar) — run without it and flag; FDA-type events are US-specific.
- **Market-structure differences:**
  - Price band $2–$2000 and 400k-share volume floor are US-specific. ASSUMPTION: NSE equivalents of price ≥ Rs. 50 and 63-day average traded value ≥ Rs. 5 crore (share-count floors bias toward low-priced stocks).
  - The book mentions shorting rectangles' downside breaks [p. 6] but trades longs only in its examples; long-only fits the NSE cash market.
  - Buy-stop entries on breakout days can hit upper circuit; ASSUMPTION: if the stock opens at its upper band, no fill that day.
  - Costs: STT, brokerage and ~0.1% slippage per side matter with stops ~3% below entry (e.g. 11.25/10.85 ≈ 3.6% [p. 19]; 64.90/63.30 ≈ 2.5% [p. 28]).

## 6. Verdict

- **Codeability:** Partly. Filters, entry order, stop placement, Fibonacci targets and the two-part exit are explicit, but pattern recognition is discretionary by the author's own statement and the key intraday-confluence filter needs data we don't have.
- **Priority for backtesting:** Low. Its daily-bar core duplicates generic consolidation-breakout rules covered by other books in the queue (Minervini VCP, Ladha breakout, Dixit), its evidence is two trades, and its distinctive edge (intraday confluence) can't be tested.
- **Top 3 things a coder is most likely to get wrong**
  1. Placing the initial stop at a fixed percentage: it goes slightly below the pattern's **rising support line**, so stop distance varies per pattern [p. 19, 28].
  2. Projecting the Fibonacci extension from the swing high instead of from the **retracement point** after it [p. 29].
  3. Leaving a pending buy-stop after price has hit the planned stop level: the order must be **cancelled** because the pattern is invalidated [p. 22–23].
