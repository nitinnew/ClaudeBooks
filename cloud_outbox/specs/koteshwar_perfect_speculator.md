## 1. The method in brief

This sequel to *The Perfect Stock* sets out the fictional speculator "Boyd Hunt's" rules explicitly:

1. Trade only when the indices are in a confirmed weekly uptrend (higher highs and higher lows) and at least two "20/4" leaders from two industries have appeared [p. 70, 104].
2. Buy "true breakouts" of young stocks (≤ 10–15 years listed) to all-time highs that satisfy all of the following [p. 65–68, 83, 93, 126]:
   - after a years-long base, an uptrend into all-time highs and then a tight, low-volume consolidation;
   - 52-week high ≥ 2× 52-week low;
   - breakout volume huge (a day's average volume in the first hour);
   - entry by buy-stop.
3. Test with 5–10% of capital and a 10% stop; a breakaway gap gets up to 15% [p. 97, 105].
4. The position must rise ≥ 20% within 4 weeks ("20/4"); then move the stop above breakeven [p. 112–113].
5. Only then add a second stock or pyramid. Pyramid size is capped so that the worst case is breakeven on the whole trade [p. 113–115].
6. Trail the stop just below each confirmed higher low on weekly charts [p. 107–108].
7. When many leaders hit their stops, go to cash [p. 116].

Expectation: 3–4 tradable uptrends per decade, doubling capital in each [p. 64–65]. No statistics; the illustration is the TASR example.

**Pseudocode** (weekly management, daily execution)

```
regime_on = for each of NIFTY50, NIFTY500, NIFTY MIDCAP, NIFTY SMALLCAP (ASSUMPTION for Dow/S&P/Nasdaq/Transports):
              weekly last swing low > prior swing low and close > last swing high
            and count(20/4 movers in last 8 weeks, distinct sectors) >= 2
eligible(s): listing_age <= 15y and H52 >= 2*L52 and C >= 0.80*ATH
             and prior advance into ATH then base: depth (Hbase-Lbase)/Hbase <= 0.20, len >= 3 weeks
             and min(weekly vol in base) < 0.5*SMA(weekly vol, 26)
entry: buy-stop at Hbase + tick on a day with V >= 2*SMA(V,50)   # proxy for "avg daily vol in first hour" (ASSUMPTION)
size = 0.075*equity ; stop = 0.90*fill   (0.85 if breakaway, see 2.2)
if max(H) >= 1.20*fill within 20 sessions: stop = fill*1.005 ; flag 20/4 ; allow next new position
elif 20 sessions passed and not 20/4: exit at close (ASSUMPTION for "be prepared to let go")
pyramid on next base breakout P2 with new stop S2 = 0.90*P2 (raise S1 to S2): qty2 = floor(q1*(S2-fill)/(P2-S2)) ; max 2 buys
trail (weekly): when weekly high > prior swing high, stop = last weekly swing low * 0.99
regime_off: >= 50% of open 20/4 positions stopped within 4 weeks -> no new buys
```

**Ambiguities & assumptions**

- "A little above" or "a little below" isn't quantified. ASSUMPTION: ±0.5–1%.
- The first-hour volume rule needs intraday data. ASSUMPTION for daily-only tests: breakout-day volume ≥ 2× the 50-day average (the volume a stock would trade if one full average day printed in the first hour).
- The 4-week failure rule says only "be prepared to start looking at other prospects" [p. 112]. ASSUMPTION: exit at week 4 if +20% was not reached.

### 2.3 Market regime switch (when to be invested at all)

| Field | Rule (as stated by the author) | Page |
|---|---|---|
| Type | market timing filter | 54–61, 104, 116 |

## 3. Risk & money-management rules

- "First, do no harm": avoid big drawdowns, since a 33% loss needs a 50% gain to recover [p. 40].
- New entrants: go three months without buying anything [p. 103].
- Test buys only; never commit more until the first position is proven (rules 3 and 7) [p. 104–105, 113].
- Stop-losses always on, decided in advance; don't watch intraday; let the stops make the sell decisions [p. 105, 121–122, 124].
- Pyramid size capped so the worst case is breakeven overall; at most two buys [p. 114–115].
- Keep a written trade journal [p. 126].

## 4. Non-codable guidance

- Ignore media and humans; only leading stocks and indices count [p. 122–123].
- Earnings: anticipated earnings matter, past earnings lag [p. 22, 85].
- Chart patterns (cups, flags and so on) are irrelevant; read price and volume on weekly charts, and confirm on daily and monthly charts [p. 91, 93, 136].
- Weekend routine: weekly index charts, then new 52-week highs checked on all-time charts, then sister stocks, then a short list near all-time highs [p. 135–136].

## 5. Adapting to NSE (Indian equities)

- **Data required:** daily and weekly OHLCV, all-time highs, listing dates (NSE listing date as a proxy for company age), sector tags, index series. Intraday first-hour volume for the strict breakout rule (or the daily proxy).
- **Testability with our data:** Fully backtestable from 2005 with the daily-volume proxy.
- **Market-structure differences:**
  - The "young company" filter maps to NSE listing within 15 years; many IPOs since 2007 qualify.
  - Index set for the regime filter (ASSUMPTION): NIFTY 50, NIFTY 500, NIFTY Midcap 150 and NIFTY Smallcap 250 in place of Dow, S&P 500, Nasdaq and Transports.
  - Circuit limits may stop a breakaway gap from printing and cap the first-hour volume; 20% circuit stocks need a fallback.
  - Liquidity floor (ASSUMPTION): 20-day median traded value ≥ Rs. 5 crore.

## 6. Verdict

- **Codeability:** Yes. Almost every rule is quantified (≤ 15 years listed, 52-week high ≥ 2× low, base ≤ 20%, base volume < 50%, 10%/15% stops, 20/4 test, breakeven move, pyramid formula, swing-low trail, regime switch). The remaining thresholds are minor.
- **Priority for backtesting:** High. A complete, rule-based Darvas/O'Neil-style long-only breakout system with a market-regime switch and position management. It fits NSE daily data, and it pairs with specs/koteshwar_perfect_stock.md (whose exhaust-day exit can be added as a variant).
- **Top 3 things a coder is most likely to get wrong**
  1. Using 52-week-high breakouts. The rule requires all-time highs, a prior uptrend, AND 52-week high ≥ 2× 52-week low [p. 67–68].
  2. Opening a second position, or pyramiding, before the first trade's stop is above breakeven after a 20/4 move [p. 113].
  3. Sizing the pyramid as a fixed fraction instead of capping it so that a stop-out of the whole position is at worst breakeven [p. 115].
