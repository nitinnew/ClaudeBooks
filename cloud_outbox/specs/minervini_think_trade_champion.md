## 1. The method in brief

This book is the companion to *Trade Like a Stock Market Wizard* and covers the trade-management side of Minervini's SEPA® method. It says how to buy in summary and how to plan, size and sell in detail. **Buying:** only stocks in a confirmed **Stage 2** uptrend that pass all eight **Trend Template** criteria. They must be near 52-week highs with strong relative strength, and are bought as they break out through a **pivot** at the end of a **volatility contraction pattern (VCP)**, i.e. progressively smaller pullbacks with volume drying up [p. 103–118]. Variants are the **cup completion cheat (3-C)**, **low cheat**, cup-with-handle, double bottom and **power play** (high tight flag) [p. 132–141]. **Risk-first:** a stop is set before entry at no more than 10%, average losses run about half that, and the stop size is tied to one's realised average gain (≥ 2:1) [p. 45, 52–53, 68]. Risk is 1.25–2.5% of equity per trade, positions are 20–25% ideally, and the book holds 4–12 stocks [p. 143–144]. Exposure is scaled up only after wins [p. 91–92]. **After entry:** watch for follow-through ("tennis ball action") and exit early on clustered "violations" [p. 29–38]. Raise the stop to breakeven once the gain is about 3× the risk or 2× the average gain [p. 60–61, 94]. Trail with the 50-day line ("breakeven or better") [p. 162–163], or sell into strength on climax signs in late-stage bases [p. 151–160].

Evidence offered: the author's record (220%/yr average over five years, 1997 U.S. Investing Championship 155%) [p. 196]; an audit showing alpha 212%, beta 0.43, 88% positive months [p. 171]; ">95 percent" of big winners were in Stage 2 in his studies back to the late 1800s [p. 103]; a 20-day-line close soon after a VCP breakout "cut in about half" the success probability [p. 35]. These are claims, not published tests. The examples are US stocks.

**Pseudocode** (daily bars; weekly for base geometry; index proxy as below)

```
# --- Trend Template (all must be true on day t) ---
TT_t = C>SMA150 and C>SMA200 and SMA150>SMA200 and SMA200_t > SMA200_{t-21} (variant: t-84..t-105 monotone)
       and SMA50>SMA150 and SMA50>SMA200 and C >= 1.25*min(L,252) and C >= 0.75*max(H,252)
       and RSrank_t >= 70 (variant 90) and RSline_t > RSline_{t-30}                         # RS as defined below (ASSUMPTION)
       and C > SMA50
RSrank: percentile across universe of 0.4*r63 + 0.2*r126 + 0.2*r189 + 0.2*r252               # ASSUMPTION (IBD-style weighting; book gives none)
RSline = C / index_proxy
# --- VCP detection over base window (from the base high H0 to t, 3–65 weeks) ---
swing highs/lows (5-bar pivots) inside base → contractions c_i = (swingH_i - swingL_i)/swingH_i
VCP = 2 <= n(c) <= 6 and c_1 in [0.10, 0.40] and c_{i+1} <= 0.75*c_i for all i (ASSUMPTION: "about half ± reasonable")
      and c_last <= 0.10 and mean(V over last contraction) < SMA50(V) and min(V over last 10d) <= 0.6*SMA50(V)
pivot P = max(H over last contraction)
# --- entry ---
buy-stop at P*1.001 when TT and VCP; fill = max(O, P); skip if fill > P*1.05 ("a few percent")
require V_breakout >= 1.4*SMA50(V) (ASSUMPTION "expanding volume"; check at close, else exit next day if not met? → variant)
stop0 = fill*(1 - s), s = min(0.08, 0.5*avg_gain_realised)  (cold start s = 0.07)               # 2:1 rule, 10% cap
risk_cash = 0.0125..0.025*equity ; shares = min(risk_cash/(fill-stop0), 0.25*equity/fill)
# --- management (first ~10 sessions) ---
violations: count of {low-vol breakout & high-vol down day; 3 consecutive lower lows with V>SMA50; downdays>updays;
             bad closes (C in lower half of range) > good closes; C<SMA20; C<SMA50 & V>1.5*SMA50; full retrace of >= 2R gain}
if violations >= 2: sell half; if >= 3: sell all (ASSUMPTION thresholds)
# --- profit protection ---
if gain >= 3*s (and > avg gain): stop = max(stop, entry)
if gain >= 2*avg_gain: stop = max(stop, entry*(1+avg_gain))                                      # back stop
when SMA50 >= entry: exit on close < SMA50 (weekly-close variant)
sell-into-strength (late stage, base_count >= 4 or climax): sell when (C/C_{t-15} >= 1.25) or (up-days >= 70% of last 10)
     or largest up day since move start; sell on largest down day (or week) since Stage-2 start
```

**Ambiguities & assumptions**

- **RS ranking** is IBD's proprietary 1–99 score [p. 106]. ASSUMPTION: compute a percentile rank of weighted 3/6/9/12-month returns across our universe; RS line = price ÷ index proxy.
- **VCP geometry** ("about half (plus or minus a reasonable amount)" [p. 109]) is discretionary. ASSUMPTION: each contraction ≤ 75% of the previous, last ≤ 10%; test ≤ 60% as stricter variant. This is the main implementation risk.
- **Stop sizing** depends on one's own realised average gain (Result-Based Assumption) [p. 58–59, 68]. In a backtest this creates a feedback loop. ASSUMPTION: start at 7%, then use rolling last-50-trade average gain ÷ 2, bounded to [3%, 8%].
- **"Expanding volume" on breakout** has no number [p. 116]. ASSUMPTION: ≥ 1.4× 50-day average.
- **Violation thresholds** — the author sells "depending upon how many violations occur and how severe they are" [p. 38]. ASSUMPTION: 2 → half, 3 → all.
- **Base counting** for late-stage sells [p. 151–152] needs base segmentation. ASSUMPTION: a new base = a ≥ 3-week consolidation with depth ≥ 8% after a ≥ 20% advance; reset count after a market correction ≥ 15% in the index proxy.
- **P/E expansion** rule [p. 154–155] requires EPS history; our fundamentals start Feb 2026. ASSUMPTION: omit in backtests.
- **Holding through earnings** only with ≥ 10% cushion [p. 96–97] needs earnings dates. ASSUMPTION: omit (flag as a known difference).

**Key quotes**

- "To qualify, the stock should have already moved up by at least 25 to 100 percent—and in some cases by 200 or 300 percent—during the previous 3 to 36 months of trading." [p. 132–133]
- "The pattern can form in as few as 3 weeks to as many as 45 weeks (most are 7 to 25 weeks in duration)." [p. 133]
- "The stock will pause over a number of days or weeks and form a plateau area (the cheat), which should be contained within 5 percent to 10 percent from high point to low point." [p. 134]

**Pseudocode**

```
prior_run: max over k in [63,756] of (C_{t-base_len}/min(L_{t-base_len-k..t-base_len}) - 1) >= 0.25
cup: base high H0, base low L0 with (H0-L0)/H0 in [0.15, 0.40] (max 0.50), length 15–225 sessions
recovery: C rallies to L0 + (1/3..1/2)*(H0-L0)
plateau: >= 5 sessions with (max(H)-min(L))/max(H) in [0.05, 0.10]; V mean < SMA50(V)
entry: H_t > max(H over plateau) ; stop = min(L over plateau)*0.99 bounded at 8%
size: half of the normal 2.1 size; add the other half at the handle/base-high pivot (ASSUMPTION)
```

**Ambiguities & assumptions**

- "Recouping about one-third to one-half its previous decline" [p. 134] is a typical, not required, level. ASSUMPTION: the plateau's top lies between 25% and 66% of the cup depth above the low.
- The low cheat [p. 135–136] differs only in position (lower third). ASSUMPTION: same code with the plateau below ⅓ of cup depth; test separately.

**Key quotes**

- "An explosive price move on huge volume that propels the stock price up 100 percent or more within eight weeks." [p. 140]
- "Following the explosive move, the stock price moves sideways in a relatively tight range, not correcting more than 20 percent" [p. 140]
- "If the correction in the base, from high to low, does not exceed 10 percent, it is not necessary to see price tightening in the form of a volatility contraction" [p. 140–141]

**Pseudocode**

```
pole: exists window ending e with C_e / min(L over e-40..e) >= 2.0 and mean(V over window) >= 2*SMA50(V at window start)
flag: sessions e+1..t, length 10–30, max drawdown from max(H) <= 0.20 (0.25 if price < Rs 100), weekly close range tight
      if drawdown > 0.10: require contraction (last 5-day range < 0.5 * first 5-day range)
entry: H_t > max(H over flag) → buy-stop; stop = min(0.08, flag low) ; manage per 2.1
```

**Ambiguities & assumptions**

- "Huge volume" is unquantified [p. 140]. ASSUMPTION: pole volume ≥ 2× the prior 50-day average.
- "Some lower-priced stocks can correct as much as 25 percent" [p. 140]. ASSUMPTION: price < Rs. 100 for the 25% allowance.

## 3. Risk & money-management rules

- **Plan before entry:** entry trigger, stop, profit-taking, position size, re-entry criteria, disaster plan [p. 23–27].
- **Stop:** predetermined, never > 10%; average losses ≤ 5–6% [p. 45, 144]; "losses are a function of expected gain" — stop ≤ ½ realised average gain [p. 52–53, 68]. Do not widen stops for volatility (anti-ATR) [p. 54].
- **Tough markets:** cut stops from 7–8% to 5–6%, take profits at 10–12% instead of 15–20%, get off margin, reduce position sizes and exposure [p. 56–57].
- **Expectancy** = (PWT × AG) ÷ (PLT × AL); maintain > 1 [p. 57].
- **Risk per trade:** 1.25–2.5% of equity; positions 20–25%, ≤ 50%; 4–8 (max 10–12) stocks [p. 143–144].
- **Scaling exposure:** start with a quarter position, double after each win; scale down when losing; never increase exposure when not profitable at 25–50% invested [p. 91–92].
- **Profit protection:** move the stop to breakeven at 3× risk (and above average gain); at 2× average gain back-stop at the average gain [p. 60–61, 94]; never let a good-size gain become a loss [p. 93].
- **Sell-half rule** when undecided at ~2× average gain [p. 75–76]; never sell half on the downside — at the stop, sell all [p. 76].
- **Earnings:** don't hold a large position into a report without ~10% profit cushion [p. 96–97].
- **Never average down** ("Losers average losers") [p. 84–85]; the 50/80 rule on broken leaders [p. 85].
- **Statistics to track:** average win/loss, batting average, largest win/loss, holding days for gains vs losses [p. 72].

## 4. Non-codable guidance

- Buy in order of breakout; don't play favourites [p. 130–131].
- "Differential disclosure": avoid stocks that fall sharply on large volume despite good news [p. 88–89].
- Leaders bottom first and emerge within the first 4–8 weeks of a new bull market; watch the top 2–3 stocks in a group [p. 123–128].
- Develop "sit-out power"; don't force trades or call audibles [p. 95–99].
- Process over outcome; mental rehearsal; celebrate small losses (Section 11) [p. 180–193].

## 5. Adapting to NSE (Indian equities)

- **Data required:** daily OHLCV (MAs 20/50/150/200, 52-week high/low, volume averages); an index proxy for the RS line and regime; cross-sectional returns for RS rank; EPS for P/E-expansion (forward-only); earnings dates (not available).
- **Testability with our data:**
  - Backtestable 2005–2026 on prices: Trend Template, VCP / 3-C / power-play entries, stops, violations, breakeven/back-stop/50-day trail, climax sell rules, position sizing and exposure scaling.
  - RS: Nifty only from 2024. ASSUMPTION: RS line vs NIFTYBEES (or equal-weight panel index); RS rank computed across our ~3,700-stock panel (including delisted names).
  - Forward-test only: P/E expansion (fundamentals from Feb 2026). Not testable: earnings-proximity rule (no earnings calendar), IBD RS ranking (replaced).
- **Market-structure differences:**
  - Built for US growth stocks. The NSE cash market has no overnight shorting; the book is long-only in practice except the author's KORS short anecdote [p. 108].
  - Circuit limits: a VCP breakout can lock at the upper circuit (no fill) and gaps through stops are possible; ASSUMPTION: skip entries when the breakout day opens at the upper band; fill stops at the open on gap-downs.
  - Liquidity: the book prefers under-followed names; ASSUMPTION: require 50-day average traded value ≥ Rs. 2 crore so 20–25% positions are executable for the intended account size.
  - Costs: STT, brokerage, ~0.1% slippage; with 5–8% stops and frequent breakout fills, model 0.2–0.3% breakout slippage as a sensitivity.
  - "Market correction" thresholds for base-count resets: ASSUMPTION ≥ 15% drawdown in the index proxy.

## 6. Verdict

- **Codeability:** Partly. The Trend Template, stops, sizing and exit overlays are explicit; VCP/3-C/power-play recognition and RS rank must be approximated, and the stop rule depends on one's own realised average gain.
- **Priority for backtesting:** High. The Trend Template plus pivot breakout with tight risk control is fully testable on our price panel, NSE momentum literature makes it a strong candidate, and this book supplies the trade-management rules the first Minervini book (blocked by size) only summarises.
- **Top 3 things a coder is most likely to get wrong**
  1. Treating the Trend Template as an entry signal: it is only a filter; entry requires a VCP-type base and a pivot breakout on volume [p. 109, 116].
  2. Using fixed or ATR-widened stops: the book caps stops at 10%, wants ~5–7%, ties them to realised average gain, and explicitly rejects widening for volatility [p. 54, 68].
  3. Applying "sell into strength" rules to early-stage breakouts: the same up-day clustering is a hold signal after a first/second base following a market correction and a sell signal only from late-stage (4th–5th) bases [p. 152, 166].
