## 1. The method in brief

Kennedy teaches pure price-bar reading (no indicators, trendlines or Elliott) in three steps [p. 3, 32]:

1. **Trend from swings.** Higher highs and higher lows = up; lower highs and lower lows = down; sideways = neutral, stand aside.
2. **Natural support/resistance.** Previous highs/lows, congestion zones, and hard/soft gaps measured from the close before the gap.
3. **Bar substructure to time entries.** The close's location in the bar range, inside/outside bars, and five defined setups traded with next-bar entry and a fixed initial stop:
   - multiple inside bars;
   - double key reversal;
   - Popgun;
   - Arrow;
   - failed new low/high and "The Zone".

A **"key" level** (most recent swing) and a **"critical" level** (the swing before) act as trend-change alarms and stop-raising cues [p. 8–11]. Evidence: chart examples only (2008 futures, stocks, FX).

**Single-bar bias rules** (filters used to time 2.1–2.6)

- A close in the upper 20% of the bar range favours higher prices next session; the lower 20% favours lower [p. 22].
- Open and close both in the upper (lower) part of the range ("doji/spinning top" variant) → bias up (down) [p. 22].
- Open and close near the mid-range = indecision, common before big moves and at turning points [p. 23, 32].
- Inside bar = pause; outside bar = volatility / capitulation at turning points [p. 24].

**Parameters**

- Multiple inside bars: no fewer than 3 contained bars [p. 45].
- Inside/outside bars require strict new extremes; equality does not count [p. 46].
- Close-location threshold: upper/lower 20% of range [p. 22].
- Failed-new-low zone width = one range of the extreme bar [p. 39–40].
- S/R tolerance: "within just a few percentage points" of the prior extreme still counts [p. 12].

**Pseudocode** (2.1, 2.2, 2.3, 2.5)

```
inside(i)  = H[i] < H[i-1] and L[i] > L[i-1]          # strict (p.46)
outside(i) = H[i] > H[i-1] and L[i] < L[i-1]

# 2.1 multiple inside bars (mother bar m, >=3 children)
if all(H[j] < H[m] and L[j] > L[m] for j in m+1..t-1) and (t-1-m) >= 3:
    if C[t] > H[m]: long at open[t+1], stop = L[t-1]
    if C[t] < L[m] and trend_down: short at open[t+1], stop = H[t-1]

# 2.2 double key reversal
bull_dkr(t) = L[t] < L[t-1] and C[t] > max(C[t-1], C[t-2])   -> long open[t+1], stop L[t]
bear_dkr(t) = H[t] > H[t-1] and C[t] < min(C[t-1], C[t-2])   -> short open[t+1], stop H[t]

# 2.3 popgun (inside at t-1? ordering per text: inside bar then outside bar)
if inside(o-1) and outside(o):           # o = outside bar index
    first later close > H[o] -> long next open, stop L[o]
    first later close < L[o] -> short next open, stop H[o]
    (ASSUMPTION: signal valid for 5 bars after o)

# 2.5 failed new low
L_bar = most recent swing-low bar (ASSUMPTION: 5-bar fractal low)
zone_low = L[L_bar] - (H[L_bar]-L[L_bar])
if later bar makes low in [zone_low, L[L_bar]) and not below zone_low, then C > H[L_bar]:
    long next open, stop = L[L_bar]           # text: "low of the low bar"
# exits: not specified -> ASSUMPTION trail stop to key swing (2.7) or exit on critical-level break
```

**Ambiguities & assumptions**

- **No profit targets or exits** beyond initial stops; the key/critical swing framework is the implied trailing exit [p. 11, 29]. ASSUMPTION: trail to the key level; exit on a critical-level break.
- **Arrow rules are internally inconsistent.** Long triggers on a close above "the high of bar four" [p. 38]; short on "the close below bar three" [p. 38]; the example says "the low of bar three" [p. 39]. Bar numbering in the definition [p. 25] differs from the trading section [p. 38]. ASSUMPTION: use the outermost combined range of bars 3–4 for both directions.
- **"Failed new low" stop:** the text says "stop at the low of the low bar", meaning the original low bar, yet price has already traded below it inside the zone [p. 40]. ASSUMPTION: use the new extreme low (or zone bottom) to avoid an immediately-hit stop; test both.
- **Swing definition is visual** ("sizeable moves", "major swings that look distinct") [p. 7, 45]. A swing filter (e.g., ATR- or %-based zigzag) is an ASSUMPTION.
- **The failed-new-low example cites "Figure 5-13"**, apparently meaning 5-14 [p. 39].

## 3. Risk & money-management rules

- Every setup has a defined initial protective stop at a bar extreme (see table).
- Break of the key level → protect profits / raise stops; break of the critical level → trend change [p. 8, 11].
- Avoid trades whose stop distance is huge: "this example may not be the best, because the risk on this move would probably be quite huge and violate most money management rules." [p. 39]
- Stand aside in neutral (sideways) trends [p. 9, 19].

## 4. Non-codable guidance

- Pick a chart timeframe matching your holding horizon; trend is "a function of time" [p. 6–7, 43].
- S/R is a range rather than a line; allow false pushes and second attempts [p. 16].
- A level's significance grows with how long it has existed [p. 12].
- Elliott corrective price channels (parallel lines off extremes plus a midline) as a supplementary filter [p. 44].

## 5. Adapting to NSE (Indian equities)

- **Data required:** OHLC bars (5-min, 15-min, daily, weekly) for NIFTY/BANKNIFTY futures, liquid F&O stocks and cash equities.
- **Testability with our data:** 2.1–2.6 are mechanical bar patterns (with the swing/zone ASSUMPTIONS); 2.7 is mechanical given a swing filter. All are readily backtestable.
- **Market-structure differences:**
  - NSE daily bars often gap at the open. "Enter next bar" means the next open, so gap slippage beyond the setup level must be modelled.
  - Soft gaps (open above the prior close) are very common on NSE; Kennedy's soft-gap S/R uses the prior close [p. 14, 43].
  - Intraday, use regular-session 5-min bars (09:15–15:30) and ignore the pre-open auction bar or treat it separately (ASSUMPTION).
  - Short setups in cash equities are intraday-only; use futures for multi-day shorts.

## 6. Verdict

- **Codeability:** High for the bar patterns. They have precise definitions, next-bar entries and stops, with a few ambiguities (Arrow numbering, swing definition). No exits/targets beyond stops.
- **Priority for backtesting:** Medium. The patterns are classic, cheap to code and testable across timeframes. Pair them with a trend filter (swing structure) and a trailing exit (key/critical levels). The double key reversal and multiple-inside-bar breakout are the clearest.
- **Top 3 things a coder is most likely to get wrong**
  1. Allowing equal highs/lows in inside/outside bar definitions. Kennedy requires strict new extremes [p. 46].
  2. Entering on the breakout intrabar. Every setup requires a *close* beyond the trigger level, then entry on the next bar [p. 33, 36, 41].
  3. Counting fewer than 3 contained bars as a "multiple inside bar" setup, or trading shorts against the larger trend [p. 33, 45].
